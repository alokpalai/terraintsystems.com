Flood & Climate

Cyclone impact mapping: seeing what changed on the ground

After a cyclone, the key question is what changed and where. Satellite change detection turns large volumes of imagery into fast, precise impact maps.

TerraINT SystemsOct 20262 min read

When a cyclone strikes, knowing its track is only the start. Responders need to know what changed on the ground: which areas flooded, which roads failed, which settlements were hit hardest.

What can be mapped

  • Flooded and waterlogged areas
  • Damaged infrastructure and roads
  • Shoreline and coastal change
  • Vegetation and land-cover loss
  • High-impact and vulnerable zones
  • Exposure of critical assets and settlements

From imagery to impact intelligence

The workflow starts with pre-cyclone baseline imagery, followed by post-cyclone acquisitions. Change detection highlights where significant change has occurred, and the results are turned into clear, location-based maps.

Baseline imageryPost-event imageryChange detectionImpact maps

Why it matters

In a disaster, information must be both fast and spatially precise. Impact maps support rapid damage assessment, response prioritisation, infrastructure recovery, coastal risk assessment, insurance and long-term resilience planning.

See the impact. Prioritise the response.
