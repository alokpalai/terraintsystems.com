Flood & Climate

Is your facility ready for an unexpected flood?

Flooding can halt operations, damage critical assets and break supply chains. Here is how a geospatial flood risk assessment shows exposure before the water does.

TerraINT SystemsOct 20262 min read

For an industrial site, a flood is rarely just water on the ground. It means damaged equipment, interrupted operations, delayed shipments and expensive downtime. Most of that loss happens at sites where flood exposure was never examined closely.

The risks that are easy to overlook

  • Low-lying or flood-prone parts of a site where water collects quickly
  • Critical assets such as transformers, pumps and storage that sit below safe levels
  • Drainage that was designed for yesterday's land use and rainfall
  • Changing flow paths caused by new construction around the facility

What a geospatial assessment adds

A flood risk assessment brings several lines of evidence onto one map. Terrain and elevation analysis shows ground levels and slopes. Drainage and flow-path analysis shows where surface water will travel. Satellite data describes land use and change around the site. Finally, asset exposure mapping overlays your critical facilities on the identified hazard zones.

TerrainDrainageSatelliteAsset exposureRisk map

The result is not just a hazard map, but a clear view of which assets are exposed, by how much, and where mitigation will make the biggest difference.

Who benefits most

Power plants, manufacturing units, infrastructure projects, mining operations and logistics hubs all carry high-value assets that are hard to move. For them, understanding exposure early is far cheaper than recovering after an event.

Don't wait for a flood to reveal your vulnerabilities.
